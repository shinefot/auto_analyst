# Tesla — Auto-Analyst Note (FY2025)

## Summary

Tesla's reported financials show a business that scaled rapidly through FY2022 and has since plateaued and now contracted, with revenue falling to $94.83bn in FY2025 from $97.69bn in FY2024 (10-K, FY2025; 10-K, FY2024). The single most important change this year is the combination of a second consecutive year of margin compression with a sharp free cash flow recovery, driven almost entirely by a capex pullback rather than operating improvement. This matters because it raises the question of whether FY2025's stronger cash generation reflects genuine efficiency gains or simply reduced investment in future growth.

## What changed this year

The most severe signal is the revenue decline itself: -2.9% in FY2025 versus +0.9% in FY2024 and +18.8% in FY2023 (10-K, FY2025; FY2024; FY2023). This marks the first outright contraction in the dataset, following a deceleration trend from +70.7% (FY2021) and +51.4% (FY2022). The mechanism is not visible directly in these metrics (no unit/ASP data), but the coincident margin trends suggest both volume and pricing pressure: gross margin, having already fallen from 25.6% (FY2022) to 18.2% (FY2023), was roughly flat at 17.9–18.0% in FY2024–FY2025, while operating margin kept eroding — 16.8% (FY2022) → 9.2% (FY2023) → 7.2% (FY2024) → 4.6% (FY2025). Net margin followed the same path, falling to 4.0% (FY2025) from 7.3% (FY2024) and 15.5% (FY2023).

A second notable change is the jump in R&D intensity, from 4.1% of revenue (FY2023) to 4.6% (FY2024) to 6.8% (FY2025), alongside SBC rising from 1.9% (FY2023) to 3.0% (FY2025). With revenue shrinking, these are classic negative operating-leverage signals: fixed or growing expense bases against a smaller revenue denominator compress margins mechanically, independent of any change in underlying spend.

Third, receivables have grown every year even as revenue growth stalled: $3.51bn (FY2023) → $4.42bn (FY2024) → $4.58bn (FY2025), a rising receivables-to-revenue ratio that merits scrutiny given flat-to-declining top line.

The positive flag — free cash flow growing 73.7% to $6.22bn (FY2025) from $3.58bn (FY2024) — is real but its driver is capex discipline, not operating strength: capex fell to $8.53bn (FY2025) from $11.34bn (FY2024), pulling the capex-to-CFO ratio down to 57.8% (FY2025) from 76.0% (FY2024). Meanwhile CFO itself was essentially flat to slightly down, $14.75bn (FY2025) versus $14.92bn (FY2024).

## Cross-checks

Revenue declined while FCF rose sharply — these are not contradictory once decomposed. CFO was nearly unchanged ($14.75bn FY2025 vs $14.92bn FY2024), so the FCF improvement is a capex story, not a revenue or profitability story. This is consistent with the operating and net margin deterioration noted above: the business generated more free cash despite, not because of, its operating performance. The question for diligence is whether the FY2025 capex level ($8.53bn) represents a sustainable steady state or a deferral of investment that could pressure future growth or margins.

Gross margin held roughly steady (18.2% FY2023, 17.9% FY2024, 18.0% FY2025) even as revenue fell, while operating margin kept declining — this points to the margin pressure sitting below the gross-profit line, in opex (R&D and SBC both rose as a % of revenue) rather than in cost of goods sold. That is broadly consistent with the R&D and SBC percentage trends cited above.

Inventory was roughly flat ($13.63bn FY2023, $12.02bn FY2024, $12.39bn FY2025) against a shrinking revenue base, meaning inventory intensity (inventory relative to revenue) has risen — worth reconciling against unit delivery data not present here, since flat inventory with falling revenue could reflect either deliberate stock discipline or slower sell-through.

Cash on hand was essentially unchanged ($16.14bn FY2024, $16.51bn FY2025) despite the FCF improvement, and diluted shares crept up (3498m FY2024 to 3528m FY2025, +0.9%), suggesting the incremental free cash flow was not primarily used to shrink the share count or materially build the cash balance — its use is not observable from these metrics.

## Key questions for management

- What portion of the FY2025 revenue decline (-2.9%, 10-K FY2025) is attributable to volume versus average selling price, and how much reflects regulatory credit or mix changes not visible in these aggregated figures?
- Is the capex reduction to $8.53bn (FY2025) from $11.34bn (FY2024) a temporary pause or a structural reset of investment intensity, and what capacity or product plans does it affect?
- Why did R&D as a percentage of revenue rise to 6.8% (FY2025) from 4.6% (FY2024) in absolute dollar and program terms — is this new-project spend or a denominator effect from falling revenue?
- What is driving the steady rise in receivables ($3.51bn FY2023 to $4.58bn FY2025) relative to a flattening revenue base — financing terms, customer mix, or collection delays?
- With operating margin down to 4.6% (FY2025) from 16.8% (FY2022), which cost lines are structural versus cyclical, and what is management's margin recovery plan?
- Given diluted shares rose again to 3528m (FY2025, +0.9%), what is the capital allocation rationale for continued share issuance alongside improved free cash flow?
