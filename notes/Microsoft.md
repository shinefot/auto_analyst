# Microsoft — Auto-Analyst Note (FY2026)

## Summary
Microsoft's top line reaccelerated to 17.8% growth (10-K, FY2026) from 14.9% (10-K, FY2025), with operating margin reaching 46.8% (10-K, FY2026), the highest in the six-year series. The single most important change this year is a capital-spending surge: capex rose to $115.95bn (10-K, FY2026) from $64.55bn (10-K, FY2025), pushing capex-to-CFO to 63.4% (10-K, FY2026) from 47.4% (10-K, FY2025) and compressing free cash flow margin to 20.2% (10-K, FY2026) from 33.4% (10-K, FY2021). This matters because a business that has historically converted roughly a third of revenue into free cash flow is now converting only a fifth, even as reported profitability metrics improve — a divergence that diligence should not wave through as "growth investment" without further evidence.

## What changed this year

**Capex surge (highest severity).** Capex jumped to $115.95bn (10-K, FY2026) from $64.55bn (10-K, FY2025), a increase far outpacing revenue growth of 17.8% (10-K, FY2026). The capex-to-CFO ratio has climbed every year in the series — 26.9% (10-K, FY2021), 26.8% (10-K, FY2022), 32.1% (10-K, FY2023), 37.5% (10-K, FY2024), 47.4% (10-K, FY2025), 63.4% (10-K, FY2026) — indicating a structural shift in capital intensity rather than a one-year blip. The mechanism: even though CFO grew to $182.94bn (10-K, FY2026) from $136.16bn (10-K, FY2025), capex grew faster, so free cash flow actually fell to $66.99bn (10-K, FY2026) from $71.61bn (10-K, FY2025) despite record operating cash generation. This is consistent with heavy infrastructure build-out, but the filings alone do not specify what the capex is buying.

**Inventory build (medium severity, smaller dollar impact).** Inventory grew to $1.40bn (10-K, FY2026) from $0.94bn (10-K, FY2025), a 48.9% increase against 17.8% revenue growth. In absolute terms this is a small line item relative to the balance sheet, and it follows four years of inventory decline — $2.64bn (10-K, FY2021), $3.74bn (10-K, FY2022), $2.50bn (10-K, FY2023), $1.25bn (10-K, FY2024), $0.94bn (10-K, FY2025). The jump reverses that trend but off a low base, so its significance is more about direction than magnitude. Whether this reflects component stockpiling for AI hardware, device inventory, or something else cannot be determined from the data provided.

**Margin mix shift.** Gross margin actually declined to 67.9% (10-K, FY2026) from 68.8% (10-K, FY2025), yet operating margin rose to 46.8% (10-K, FY2026) from 45.6% (10-K, FY2025) and net margin jumped to 40.3% (10-K, FY2026) from 36.1% (10-K, FY2025). This implies opex leverage and/or below-the-line items are doing more work than gross margin expansion — worth separating operating discipline from one-off tax or non-operating effects.

**R&D intensity declining.** R&D as a percent of revenue fell to 10.7% (10-K, FY2026) from 11.5% (10-K, FY2025) and from a peak of 12.8% (10-K, FY2023), even as absolute revenue scaled up. In a period of heavy AI capex, a shrinking R&D-to-revenue ratio alongside a rising capex ratio suggests the incremental spending is going into physical/compute infrastructure rather than research headcount or software development — a mix diligence should confirm.

## Cross-checks

Revenue, CFO, and operating margin all point to a stronger business in FY2026, but free cash flow margin tells the opposite story, falling to 20.2% (10-K, FY2026) from 25.4% (10-K, FY2025) and from 33.4% (10-K, FY2021). The reconciliation is straightforward on the data given: it is entirely a capex story, not an operating or working-capital story. CFO grew 34% year over year ($182.94bn (10-K, FY2026) vs $136.16bn (10-K, FY2025)), which would normally support higher free cash flow, but capex grew 79.6% over the same period, more than offsetting the operating cash gain.

Cash on hand also appears to conflict with the CFO trend: cash fell to $20.94bn (10-K, FY2026) from $30.24bn (10-K, FY2025) even though CFO reached a series high. Given capex alone consumed $115.95bn (10-K, FY2026), the cash decline is plausible without invoking any other explanation, but the data provided does not itemize financing outflows (buybacks, dividends, debt repayment), so this cannot be fully reconciled from the numbers here.

Receivables growth of roughly 15.7% (69.90bn (10-K, FY2025) to 80.88bn (10-K, FY2026)) is modestly below revenue growth of 17.8% (10-K, FY2026), which does not raise a red flag on its own — if anything it suggests collections have kept pace with or outpaced sales growth, unlike the inventory line.

## Key questions for management

What specific assets is the $115.95bn (10-K, FY2026) capex being deployed into, and what is management's internal return threshold or payback horizon for this spend relative to the $64.55bn (10-K, FY2025) base?

Given capex-to-CFO has risen every year from 26.9% (10-K, FY2021) to 63.4% (10-K, FY2026), does management expect this ratio to plateau, and at what level, before free cash flow margin stabilizes?

What is driving the inventory increase to $1.40bn (10-K, FY2026) from $0.94bn (10-K, FY2025) — is this AI-hardware-related, device inventory, or something else, and should the market expect further builds?

Net margin rose to 40.3% (10-K, FY2026) from 36.1% (10-K, FY2025) despite gross margin declining to 67.9% (10-K, FY2026) from 68.8% (10-K, FY2025) — how much of this improvement is operating leverage versus tax rate, interest income, or other non-operating items?

R&D-to-revenue fell to 10.7% (10-K, FY2026) from 12.8% (10-K, FY2023) — is this a deliberate reallocation toward capex-funded infrastructure, and does it change the competitive R&D posture versus prior years?

Cash fell to $20.94bn (10-K, FY2026) from $30.24bn (10-K, FY2025) despite record CFO — how much of that decline is buybacks, dividends, and debt activity, none of which are broken out in this data set?

## Data limitations

This analysis relies solely on high-level XBRL income statement, cash flow, and select balance sheet tags from 10-K filings. It cannot see capex by category (cloud infrastructure vs. R&D facilities vs. other), segment-level revenue or margin detail, the composition or aging of receivables and inventory, financing activity detail (buybacks, dividends, debt issuance/repayment), tax rate drivers, one-time items, or forward guidance. It also cannot assess return on the capex being deployed, competitive context, or management's stated rationale for the spending mix shift — all of which would typically be found in MD&A narrative, earnings call commentary, or segment disclosures rather than the XBRL facts used here.
