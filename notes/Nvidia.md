# Nvidia — Auto-Analyst Note (FY2026)

## Summary
Nvidia's revenue scaled from $16.68B (10-K, FY2021) to $215.94B (10-K, FY2026), with the most recent year still growing 65.5% (10-K, FY2026) off a base that had already grown 114.2% (10-K, FY2025). The single most important change this year is the combination of a gross margin decline to 71.1% from 75.0% (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025) alongside inventory growing 112.3% against revenue growth of 65.5% (10-K, FY2026) — margin compression occurring simultaneously with aggressive inventory build. This matters because it raises the question of whether Nvidia is pricing more competitively to defend share, absorbing higher-cost/new-generation product mix, or building inventory ahead of demand it cannot fully guarantee.

## What changed this year
The most consequential shift is the gross margin decline, from 75.0% (10-K, FY2025) to 71.1% (10-K, FY2026), a 3.9-point drop per the flag. Mechanically, gross margin compression can come from product mix (new architecture ramp costs before yield maturity), pricing pressure, or higher input costs; the filings alone don't disaggregate this, but the magnitude is large enough on a $215.94B revenue base (10-K, FY2026) to matter for unit economics diligence. Operating margin also slipped, to 60.4% from 62.4% (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025), moving in the same direction as gross margin, which suggests the pressure originated above the operating-expense line rather than from opex creep — R&D as a percentage of revenue actually continued to fall, to 8.6% from 9.9% (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025).

The second flag, inventory growing 112.3% versus revenue growth of 65.5% (10-K, FY2026), shows inventory outpacing sales growth by roughly 1.7x. In dollar terms inventory rose to $21.40B from $10.08B (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025), while cash rose more modestly to $10.60B from $8.59B (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025). This could reflect deliberate staging for a next-generation product ramp (a plausible explanation given Nvidia's history of rapid platform transitions) or could signal that sell-through is not keeping pace with build — the filings alone cannot distinguish these.

The third flag is a positive one: diluted share count fell 1.2% (10-K, FY2026), continuing a trend from 24,940 (10-K, FY2024) to 24,804 (10-K, FY2025) to 24,514 million shares (10-K, FY2026), consistent with buyback activity that is modestly accretive to per-share metrics even as headline margins compress.

## Cross-checks
Revenue and cash generation do not tell conflicting stories this year — both grew strongly, with CFO rising to $102.72B from $64.09B (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025), a 60% increase that outpaced the 65.5% revenue growth only slightly less. FCF also grew, to $96.68B from $60.85B (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025), but FCF margin edged down to 44.8% from 46.6% (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025), and capex intensity rose, with capex-to-CFO climbing to 5.9% from 5.0% (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025). So the cash-flow engine remains intact, but it is decelerating slightly relative to revenue at the same time gross margin is compressing — these two signals are consistent with each other rather than contradictory, both pointing to the same underlying margin story.

Receivables also grew fast, to $38.47B from $23.06B (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025), a roughly 67% increase that is in line with the 65.5% revenue growth (10-K, FY2026), so receivables growth alone does not appear disconnected from sales. The more notable divergence is inventory (+112.3%) growing at nearly double the rate of both revenue (+65.5%) and receivables (~67%) (10-K, FY2026) — this is the one metric moving distinctly out of step with the rest of the working-capital picture and deserves the most scrutiny.

Worth noting for context: today's margin softness looks mild next to FY2023, when gross margin fell to 56.9% and operating margin to 15.7% (10-K, FY2023) amid a demand air-pocket. The current gross margin of 71.1% (10-K, FY2026) remains far above that trough and above FY2021's 62.3% (10-K, FY2021) — so this is a deceleration from a very high base, not a return to prior cyclical weakness, at least based on margin levels alone.

## Key questions for management
What specifically drove the 3.9-point gross margin decline this year (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025) — is it product mix, ramp costs on new architectures, competitive pricing, or input cost inflation, and is it expected to stabilize or continue?

Given inventory grew 112.3% versus revenue growth of 65.5% (10-K, FY2026), what is the composition of that inventory build (raw materials, work-in-process, finished goods) and what demand visibility supports holding it at that level?

With capex-to-CFO ratio rising to 5.9% from 5.0% (10-K, FY2026 vs FY2025), what is the multi-year capital intensity plan, and how does that interact with the gross margin trajectory?

Receivables grew roughly in line with revenue (10-K, FY2026) — are there any changes in customer concentration, payment terms, or credit risk embedded in that growth that the aggregate number would not reveal?

R&D as a percentage of revenue has fallen every year since FY2023 (27.2% to 8.6%, 10-K, FY2023 vs FY2026) even as absolute dollars presumably rose — is this operating leverage sustainable, or does it risk under-investing relative to the pace of platform transitions in the industry?

With diluted share count declining modestly (10-K, FY2024–FY2026), what is the capital allocation framework balancing buybacks against the capex intensity increase noted above?

## Data limitations
The filings provide aggregate revenue, margin, and balance-sheet figures but no segment-, product-, or geography-level breakdown, so we cannot attribute the gross margin decline or inventory build to a specific product line, customer, or region. We cannot see order backlog, channel inventory (as opposed to Nvidia's own reported inventory), or customer-level concentration, all of which would help distinguish deliberate ramp-staging from a demand slowdown. The data also does not include qualitative disclosures (MD&A commentary, risk factors) that typically explain the drivers behind margin and inventory moves — this note is based solely on the quantitative XBRL figures provided.
