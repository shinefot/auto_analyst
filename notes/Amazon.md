# Amazon — Auto-Analyst Note (FY2025)

## Summary
Amazon's FY2025 revenue reached $716.92bn, up 12.4% from $637.96bn (10-K, FY2024), with operating margin expanding to 11.2% (10-K, FY2025) from 10.8% (10-K, FY2024) — the core retail and cloud businesses appear to be scaling profitably. The single most important change this year is a sharp capex surge: capital expenditure jumped to $131.82bn (10-K, FY2025) from $83.00bn (10-K, FY2024), consuming 94.5% of operating cash flow versus 71.6% the prior year (10-K, FY2024), and compressing free cash flow to just $7.70bn (10-K, FY2025) from $32.88bn (10-K, FY2024). This matters because it converts a business that had been generating substantial discretionary cash into one that is once again investment-constrained, echoing the 2021-2022 buildout cycle, and raises the question of what return profile this new spending wave is targeting.

## What changed this year
The dominant signal is the capex-to-CFO ratio spike to 94.5% in FY2025 from 71.6% in FY2024 (10-K, FY2024; FY2025). Operating cash flow itself grew healthily, from $115.88bn (10-K, FY2024) to $139.51bn (10-K, FY2025), so this is not a cash-generation problem — it is a decision to reinvest almost all of that cash back into the business. Capex nearly doubled year over year ($83.00bn to $131.82bn), which mechanically compresses free cash flow even as the underlying operating engine strengthens. FCF margin fell to 1.1% (10-K, FY2025) from 5.2% (10-K, FY2024), the lowest level since the negative-FCF years of 2021 (-3.1%) and 2022 (-3.3%) (10-K, FY2021; FY2022).

A secondary, less severe signal is the sharp rise in receivables, from $55.45bn (10-K, FY2024) to $67.73bn (10-K, FY2025), a 22.1% increase that outpaces the 12.4% revenue growth (10-K, FY2025). This could reflect a growing mix of AWS enterprise billing with longer payment terms, but on the data alone it is simply a faster-than-revenue increase in a working-capital line.

A third, favorable signal is continued margin expansion: gross margin rose to 50.3% (10-K, FY2025) from 48.9% (10-K, FY2024), and operating margin rose to 11.2% from 10.8% over the same period, while stock-based compensation as a percent of revenue fell to 2.7% (10-K, FY2025) from 3.5% (10-K, FY2024) and 4.2% (10-K, FY2023) — suggesting some dilution/cost discipline is improving even as capex is not.

## Cross-checks
Revenue growth, gross margin, operating margin, and CFO all point to a business getting structurally more profitable and cash-generative: CFO of $139.51bn (10-K, FY2025) is the highest in the dataset, well above $46.33bn in 2021 and $46.75bn in 2022 (10-K, FY2021; FY2022) when the company was last in a heavy capex cycle. Yet FCF tells the opposite short-term story, falling to $7.70bn (10-K, FY2025), close to the depressed levels of FY2021 and FY2022. These are not contradictory once capex is isolated as the driver — the operating business is stronger than it was in 2021-2022 (operating margin 11.2% now vs 5.3% then (10-K, FY2021)), but the current capex intensity (94.5% of CFO) is even higher than in FY2022 (136.1%) in absolute dollar terms, even if the ratio is lower, because CFO itself is so much larger. The diligence question is whether this capex is funding capacity that will lift future revenue and margin (as the 2021-2022 cycle arguably did, given the subsequent margin recovery to 47.0%+ gross margin by FY2023) or whether it reflects a less certain, less demand-backed investment thesis (e.g., AI infrastructure with uncertain payback timing) — the filings do not disclose enough asset-level detail to distinguish these from the aggregate numbers alone.

Separately, net margin of 10.8% (10-K, FY2025) and diluted share count growth of only 1.0% (10-K, FY2025) suggest reported profitability is not being diluted away, even though FCF per share is compressed this year — another reconciliation point: GAAP profitability and cash generation are diverging from free-cash-flow-per-share economics purely due to the capex line, not from earnings quality or share issuance.

## Key questions for management
What is the expected useful life and revenue contribution timeline of the assets underlying the jump in capex from $83.00bn (10-K, FY2024) to $131.82bn (10-K, FY2025), and how much is AWS/AI-related versus fulfillment or other segments? Given that the FY2021-2022 capex cycle preceded margin compression (operating margin fell to 2.4% in FY2022 (10-K, FY2022)) before recovering, what specifically differs about the current cycle's risk profile? Why did receivables grow 22.1% (10-K, FY2025) versus revenue growth of 12.4% (10-K, FY2025) — is this concentrated in AWS enterprise contracts, and what is the collections experience? Is the current 94.5% capex-to-CFO ratio (10-K, FY2025) expected to persist, and if so, for how many fiscal years, and what is management's minimum FCF margin tolerance? How much of the gross margin improvement to 50.3% (10-K, FY2025) is mix-driven (e.g., higher-margin AWS/advertising revenue share) versus cost efficiency, and is it durable if capex-driven capacity comes online with associated depreciation increases?

## Data limitations
The dataset provides no segment-level breakdown (AWS versus retail versus advertising), so it is not possible to determine which businesses are driving the capex surge, the receivables growth, or the margin expansion. There is no balance sheet detail beyond receivables, inventory, and cash, so leverage, debt maturity, and lease obligations (relevant given capex-heavy build-outs) cannot be assessed. R&D as a percent of revenue is not populated (NaN across all years), preventing any read on innovation spend trends. The filings also do not disclose forward capex guidance, asset useful lives, or return-on-invested-capital by initiative, all of which are necessary to judge whether the FY2025 capex surge is value-accretive.
